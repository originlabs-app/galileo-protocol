After a luxury repair, send the item back with a record of the approved work, actual interventions, replaced parts, remaining defects and applicable service warranty. Give the next owner enough evidence to understand the repair without presenting that record as proof of authenticity or future resale value.

A collection receipt can show that an object left the workshop. It may still leave a buyer unable to tell whether the strap was replaced, the movement serviced or a cosmetic treatment declined. The useful handoff answers those questions while the workshop can still resolve them.

The following method is a practical recommendation for after-sales teams and resellers. The worked example is fictional; the named providers describe their own services and markets.

## Separate the digital service from the physical work

In its [16 November 2023 explanation of its digital product passport](https://auraconsortium.com/insight/unlocking-luxury-digital-product-passport-transforms-the-luxury-industry), Aura Blockchain Consortium describes electronic warranty and repair-assistance uses. These are the supplier's stated uses, not evidence that every participating brand offers every service.

A digital product passport is a record linked to a product. It can help carry information, but someone still has to describe what happened in the workshop. A link labelled repair history is not enough if the underlying document only says service completed.

The physical scope also varies. [Hermès' US maintenance and repair page](https://www.hermes.com/us/en/content/278343-maintenance-repair/) describes leather interventions such as zipper replacement and seam repair alongside separate watch services. That page supports those examples, not a promise that any requested intervention will be accepted or covered by warranty.

[Audemars Piguet's GB service page](https://www.audemarspiguet.com/gb/en/services/all-services) gives a concrete boundary: the warranty for its water-resistance service is limited to the replaced components. The page also distinguishes an archive extract from an authenticity certificate requiring examination of the watch.

These are different kinds of evidence: a supplier describes digital uses, while maisons describe physical services and document boundaries. The AP and Hermès pages are undated and were checked on 7 September 2026. Keep the provider, country and specific service attached to any terms you reuse.

## Build the record as the job moves through the workshop

Do not leave the whole form until collection. Each stage should leave the next person a clear decision and the evidence behind it. The following flow is a proposed working method, not a brand's published process.

### At intake, anchor the record to the object

Give the job a reference distinct from the product identifier. Record the visible identifiers, model description, accessories received and photographs of the relevant areas. If an identifier cannot be read, say so and explain how the workshop matched the item to the job.

Separate the customer's report from the workshop's observation. Record “the customer reports that the clasp opens during use” separately from “the workshop reproduced the fault during [named check]”. For a watch, name the test and its result rather than writing “works perfectly”. For a bag, mark existing wear before authorising changes.

If a tag or marked component is affected, preserve the old reference and explain the replacement link. Do not silently assign a new identity to the whole object. Our [identifier analysis](/blog/2026-09-02-chanel-serial-code-not-identity) covers why a number alone cannot establish authenticity.

### Before work, preserve what the customer approved

Link the diagnosis to a dated estimate and the accepted scope. Record who approved it through the workshop's normal customer channel. Keep the full approval in the private job file; the later buyer needs the scope, not the customer's email address.

If the diagnosis changes, obtain the required approval for the revised work before proceeding under your agreed process. Keep declined treatments visible. A strap replacement does not authorise recolouring the bag, and an approved movement service should not silently become a cosmetic restoration.

### During repair, distinguish installed, removed and untouched parts

Describe each intervention and its date. For every replacement, identify the part and the supporting reference available from the workshop. Use “maker-supplied” only when there is evidence for that description. If origin is unconfirmed, record that limit instead of upgrading it to “original”.

Record what happened to the removed part: returned, retained or disposed of under the agreed terms. Do not promise its return if the service terms provide otherwise. Note untouched components where a reader might otherwise infer a complete overhaul.

Keep the completion note separate from the estimate. An estimate shows intended work; the completion note states what the workshop actually did. If they differ, explain the change and point to its approval.

### At collection, explain the result and its boundaries

Record the final checks, their author and the date. Describe the condition at return, including remaining marks, declined work and any issue awaiting follow-up. Let the customer compare the object with the written scope before acknowledging receipt.

Copy or attach the actual service warranty terms with their provider and market. Include what is covered, the applicable dates, exclusions and the route for a claim. Check any conditions for a later owner rather than assuming coverage transfers.

If the terms are missing, mark warranty documentation pending and ask the provider for it. Do not turn missing paperwork into a promise of coverage or a claim that no rights exist. This form does not determine the customer's legal rights.

## A filled repair record: fictional bag, fictional workshop

**The workshop, item, dates, records and service promise below are fictional.** They show how to describe a limited repair and its unresolved warranty question.

Consider a resale team receiving a navy leather shoulder bag after an independent workshop replaced its strap. Here is the accompanying record.

- **Job and item:** DEMO-R17, workshop Atelier Example, unbranded navy shoulder bag, internal item reference DEMO-BAG-04. No maker serial number recorded. Intake photographs P01 to P04 link the bag to the job; they are fictional references, not downloadable evidence.
- **Intake, 1 September 2026:** customer reports a split strap edge. Technician A observes a split beside one attachment and existing scuffs on both lower corners. Bag, strap and dust bag received. No authenticity assessment requested or performed.
- **Diagnosis and approval, 2 September:** replace the strap; leave the bag body and hardware unchanged. Customer accepts estimate E01 through the private job file. Corner recolouring is offered and declined. No additional work is authorised.
- **Intervention, 4 September:** Technician A fits a workshop-supplied leather strap, part reference DEMO-S02. The record makes no maker-origin claim. Original attachment hardware is reused. The removed strap is placed in a separate pouch for return, as agreed.
- **Completion evidence:** job note J01 records the replacement; fictional photographs P05 and P06 show both attachments. The work matches E01. No cleaning, recolouring or lining repair is recorded.
- **Return check, 5 September:** Technician B records that both strap attachments close and that the bag opens and closes during a manual check. No load test or material analysis is recorded. Corner scuffs remain. This is a limited check, not a durability certification.
- **Fictional written service promise:** Atelier Example covers defects in its strap fitting from 5 September to 5 December 2026 under document W01. Material wear, the bag body and unrelated components are excluded from this invented promise. Claims go through the workshop using DEMO-R17. Transfer to a later owner is not specified and needs written confirmation. These terms are illustrative, not recommended legal wording.
- **Collection and onward copy:** customer acknowledges receiving the bag, removed strap and summary on 5 September. Contact and payment details remain in the private file. The resale copy includes the job summary and relevant warranty terms, with personal details removed.
- **Open point:** the resale coordinator must ask Atelier Example whether W01 can benefit the buyer. Until confirmed, the listing can describe the documented strap replacement but should not promise transferable repair cover.

The buyer can now distinguish a replaced strap from a restored bag. The record still does not establish ownership, authentic maker origin or today's condition. The reseller should inspect the item on arrival and disclose the replacement without claiming a resale premium.

## Reusable form for the next job

Copy these fields into your existing repair system or a document the customer can retain. Use not recorded, not applicable or pending confirmation when accurate. A blank field should not look like a successful check.

1. **Item and job:** job reference; product reference and its location; description; intake date; accessories; photographs; any identification uncertainty.
2. **Intake and diagnosis:** customer-reported issue; workshop observations; test method and result; assessor; proposed intervention.
3. **Authority to proceed:** estimate version; approved scope and date; private approval reference; declined work; later changes and their approval.
4. **Work performed:** date; workshop and technician reference; intervention; difference from the estimate; supporting completion record.
5. **Parts:** removed part; installed part and origin evidence; reused components; removed-part destination and agreement; affected identifier and replacement link, if any.
6. **Return assessment:** checks and results; reviewer and date; remaining defects; work not performed; photographs; care advice tied to the intervention.
7. **Service warranty:** provider; country; service; terms document and version; covered work or parts; dates; exclusions; transfer conditions; claims route; unanswered questions.
8. **Handoff and correction:** receipt date; attachments supplied; shareable copy; private information removed; contact for corrections; person responsible for pending follow-up.

An exportable file makes the record usable if the customer changes phone or platform. If you issue a corrected version, retain the earlier one in the job file and identify the correction. Do not make a changed account of the work indistinguishable from the original.

## At resale, assess the item again

Treat the workshop record as evidence from its stated author. Confirm unclear points through a known workshop contact, especially where the document and physical condition disagree. An invoice alone may not settle part origin or the exact work completed.

Keep three conclusions separate in the listing: the documented intervention, the reseller's present condition assessment and any independently supported authenticity finding. If the workshop only replaced a strap, avoid language implying it authenticated or restored the entire bag.

A digital passport could carry the summary or a controlled link to the supporting documents. Decide first who can issue corrections, which evidence remains accessible and what the buyer receives. The underlying repair evidence matters more than the choice of storage format.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should accompany a luxury item after repair?

Our recommended handoff includes item identification, intake condition, approved work, actual interventions, replaced parts, return checks, unresolved issues and the applicable service warranty. Keep supporting documents available and separate customer contact details from the record shared with a later buyer.

### Does a repair record prove authenticity or resale value?

No. It records what a stated workshop says it did to an identified item. Authenticity, ownership, present condition and resale valuation require their own evidence. A replacement part should be described without implying that the whole item has been authenticated.

### Does a repair restart the warranty on the whole product?

Do not assume that it does. Check the provider, country, service and written terms for that job. Record covered work or components, dates, exclusions and the claims route. If terms are missing, request them instead of inventing coverage or a duration.

### Does this form require a digital product passport?

No. The proposed method can use a paper record or an exportable digital file. A passport may carry or reference the history, but the record still needs an identifiable author and supporting evidence.
